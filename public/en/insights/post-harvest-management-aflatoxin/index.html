Post-harvest Management and Aflatoxin Contamination

A three-year study of Azerbaijani hazelnuts, published in 2026, finds that drying, more than storage, determines aflatoxin risk. Key findings for buyers and producers.

Aflatoxins, toxic compounds produced mainly by the mould Aspergillus flavus, are the most important food safety risk for tree nuts and are strictly regulated in the EU and other import markets. A study published in January 2026 in the journal Toxins examined how post-harvest handling affects this risk, using hazelnuts from Azerbaijan over three years.

Key findings

  • Drying is the decisive factor. Drying efficiency was the primary driver of fungal persistence and aflatoxin risk. Proper drying significantly reduced contamination regardless of storage conditions.
  • Storage matters less when drying is right. With adequate drying, storage temperature and shell condition had only a minimal influence.
  • Traditional sun-drying carries the highest risk. Drying on the ground or under variable weather was associated with elevated risk, while mechanical drying and well-controlled storage significantly reduced contamination.

A companion study in Frontiers in Plant Science (2026) found low aflatoxin levels in orchards at harvest, but markedly higher contamination in late-harvested nuts, particularly those collected from the ground. The authors point to orchard microclimate and harvest timing as the main pre-harvest drivers and recommend Good Agricultural Practices, timely harvest and rapid post-harvest drying.

What it means for buyers

The research confirms that aflatoxin control starts long before a container is loaded. When qualifying a hazelnut supplier, buyers should ask how and how quickly nuts are dried after harvest, how storage is controlled, and how every lot is tested before shipment.

At Bionut, harvest, processing and packing take place within one integrated operation. Raw material is inspected on arrival, every lot is checked in our in-house laboratory, and full traceability links each shipment back to the orchard, all under an FSSC 22000 and ISO 22000:2018 certified food safety system.
