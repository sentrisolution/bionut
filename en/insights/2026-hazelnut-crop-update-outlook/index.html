2026 Hazelnut Crop Update & Outlook

Türkiye's crop forecast, export performance and stock outlook, plus harvest reports from Italy, the USA and Chile — what the 2026/27 season looks like for buyers.

The International Nut & Dried Fruit Council (INC) published its latest hazelnut crop update in November 2026, based on information gathered between mid-September and October. Here is a summary of the key figures and what they mean for buyers.

Türkiye: a slightly smaller forecast

The Black Sea Hazelnut Exporters' Association trimmed its 2026 crop estimate to 803,770 MT (in-shell basis), down from 805,246 MT in late July, after further orchard assessments of kernel size and yield. The kernel recovery ratio is estimated at 50%.

  • 2025/26 exports: 197,085 MT kernel equivalent — 36% below 2024/25 — worth US$2.51 billion.
  • September 2026 (first month of the new season): 18,929 MT exported, up 36% year on year, valued at US$192.1 million.
  • TMO purchases: around 77,000 MT in-shell bought so far, with a further 100,000 MT scheduled for delivery.
  • Ending stocks 2026/27: tentatively 270,000 MT, as exports have been moving more slowly than usual.

Italy, USA and Chile

Italy proved resilient despite an exceptionally long, record-hot summer, with a crop estimated above average. In the USA, the Oregon harvest started in September in excellent weather; the crop looks slightly larger than forecast, with normal sizing and excellent kernel quality. In Chile, winter and spring conditions were favourable, and new orchards entering production should lift the 2027 crop.

What it means for buyers

A comfortable stock position in Türkiye and solid crops in Italy and the USA point to a well-supplied 2026/27 season, with confectionery demand rebuilding after the high prices of 2025. For manufacturers, it is a good moment to diversify origins and secure consistent, traceable supply. Azerbaijani hazelnuts — grown in the Gabala region and processed under certified food safety systems — offer a reliable complement to traditional origins.
